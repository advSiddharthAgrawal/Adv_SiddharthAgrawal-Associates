Codified Director Duties & Liabilities Under Section 166 of Companies Act 2013

  • By Adv. Siddharth D. Agrawal
  • August 10, 2026
  • 6 Min Read
  • Print Share

Table of Contents

  2. Statutory Duties Under Section 166
  3. Breach Liabilities and Safe Harbor Standards
  4. Conclusion & Compliance Summary

The regulatory landscape under the Companies Act 2013 has significantly increased the oversight and accountability of corporate directors in India. Codified for the first time, director duties are no longer governed solely by common law precedents, but by explicit statutory provisions.

For independent directors and executive promoters alike, understanding these codified expectations represents the baseline safeguard against personal financial liability or disqualifications.

Statutory Duties Under Section 166

Section 166 of the Companies Act 2013 lists seven fundamental duties required of every director in an Indian company. A director must:

  • Act in accordance with the company's Articles of Association (AOA).
  • Act in good faith in order to promote the objects of the company for the benefit of its members as a whole, and in the best interests of the company, its employees, the shareholders, the community and for the protection of environment.
  • Exercise his duties with due and reasonable care, skill and diligence and shall exercise independent judgment.
  • Not involve in a situation in which he may have a direct or indirect interest that conflicts, or possibly may conflict, with the interest of the company.
  • Not achieve or attempt to achieve any undue gain or advantage either to himself or to his relatives, partners, or associates.
  • Not assign his office and any assignment so made shall be void.

Breach Liabilities and Safe Harbor Standards

Failure to adhere to the standards outlined under Section 166 can trigger significant penalties. Under Section 166(7), any breach of duties is punishable by a fine which shall not be less than Rs. 1,00,000, but which may extend to Rs. 5,00,000.

Furthermore, if a director is found to have made undue gains, they can be ordered to deposit an equivalent amount back to the company. To secure "Safe Harbor" protection, independent directors must demonstrate they acted with diligence and that breaches occurred without their consent or active participation.

Conclusion & Compliance Summary

Corporate boards must establish strict compliance review channels. From ensuring that interested director declarations are recorded during meetings to structuring formal board minutes, procedural documentation is the key shield against regulatory prosecutions.
